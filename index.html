The Price of Europe

By Mojtaba Alehosseini  ·  Master's in Computer Science (AI)  ·  University of Genova, 2025–2026

How inflation reshaped daily life across 27 countries

Economic hardship is among the most pervasive forms of human suffering. Across Europe, the inflation crisis of 2020–2024 eroded wages, made food unaffordable, and pushed millions toward housing insecurity. This project maps that cost — country by country, year by year.

ACT I

The Calm Before the Storm

When Inflation Was Just a Number

2019: the last year of normal prices

The Inflation Map of Europe

In 2019, most EU countries sat near the ECB's 2% inflation target. Press play, or drag the slider, to scrub through the next six years and watch the map shift from green to red.

By mid-2022 almost every country has tipped red. Inflation runs above 10% in energy-dependent economies. The hardest hits: the Baltics, Hungary, and Czechia.

Hover any country to see its full timeline in the corner panel. Click to pin it. The dashed gold ring marks the worst-hit country at the current month.

Source: Eurostat HICP (prc_hicp_manr) — Monthly rate of annual change, all items (CP00)

Not All Prices Are Equal

The headline number hides what's underneath. Split inflation by category and the picture is much sharper: energy shot up, food followed, services barely moved.

Each panel shows one category, with countries faded behind the bold euro-area average. Hover any country line to follow the same country across every panel.

Drag a range on the overview strip below to zoom every panel into a shared time window — and click outside the selection to reset.

Source: Eurostat HICP (prc_hicp_manr) — by COICOP category

ACT II

The First Shock — COVID

Supply Chains Snapped

2020–2021: the first cracks appeared

Who Suffered Most?

One row per country, one column per category. Red means high inflation, blue means deflation, white sits at the ECB's 2% target. Sort by any column to rank countries by energy, food, or services pain.

Drag the time slider to compare 2019, the 2022 peak, and today. Eastern Europe runs hotter than the West across nearly every column — France and Germany stay close to the ECB target while Hungary, Estonia, and Slovakia don't.

Source: Eurostat HICP (prc_hicp_manr)

ACT III

The Energy Crisis

When Gas Prices Exploded

2022: the world changed overnight

Electricity: 2019 vs 2024

Household electricity doubled or tripled in some countries. Each line links a country's 2019 price to its 2024 price; the steeper the line, the bigger the bill shock.

The dashed gold line marks the EU median — anything above it is worse than typical. Switch between €/kWh and an index pinned to 2019=100 to compare absolute prices and relative jumps.

Source: Eurostat nrg_pc_204 — Electricity prices for household consumers, band DC

Timeline of Crisis

Euro-area inflation with the events that moved it. The shaded bands cover the COVID period (grey) and the energy crisis (orange). Filter by category to isolate one storyline.

The ECB rate hikes (gold) only start in mid-2022 — months after inflation had already crossed 7%. By the time policy reacted, the spike was well under way.

Source: Eurostat HICP (prc_hicp_manr) + manual event timeline

What Drove Inflation?

The same euro-area number, broken into the categories beneath it. Energy ran the show in 2022, food took the baton in 2023, services held it through 2024.

The gold dot marks the headline peak. Switch between stacked, stream, and 100% views to see contribution, shape, and share. Hover any layer to read its value at that month.

Source: Eurostat HICP (prc_hicp_manr) — Euro Area aggregate

ACT IV

Food & Wages

The Wage-Price Divergence

2023: did salaries keep up?

Did Wages Keep Up?

For most workers, no. The bars show real wage change — what you actually take home after inflation chews through nominal pay. Green: wages beat inflation. Red: purchasing power slipped.

Six countries have no statutory minimum wage and rely on sectoral bargaining (Denmark, Sweden, Finland, Austria, Italy, Cyprus). Their bars are shown faded.

Source: Eurostat earn_mw_cur + prc_hicp_aind

ACT V

The Aftermath

A New Normal?

2024–2025: the long shadow of inflation

What €100 Buys Today

One hundred squares, each one a euro. The gold ones are what €100 from 2019 still buys today; the grey ones are gone, eaten by inflation.

Pick a country and a category — the big number tweens from €100 down to the actual value. A small line below tells you whether you fared better or worse than the EU average. In parts of Eastern Europe €100 from 2019 now stretches to less than €75 of the same shopping basket.

Source: Eurostat HICP Index (prc_hicp_midx) — 2015=100

Architecture

This project uses a single-page scrollytelling architecture in which each visualization corresponds to a distinct narrative section, accessible directly via the navigation links above — the functional equivalent of the multi-page structure described in the course brief.

Data Processing Pipeline

All data was fetched from the Eurostat JSON API, parsed from the standard Eurostat dimension-value format, filtered to EU-27 countries plus the Euro Area aggregate, and exported as JSON for D3 consumption. Missing values (marked with ":" in Eurostat) were dropped. Where API data was unavailable, realistic synthetic data was generated based on known historical patterns.

Limitations & Biases

HICP vs. national CPI: HICP uses harmonized methodology, which may differ from what citizens actually experience. Reporting lag: Some countries submit data 2–3 months late; provisional data may be revised. Missing minimum wage data: 6 EU countries have no statutory minimum wage — Denmark, Sweden, Finland, Austria, Italy, and Cyprus. Currency effects: Non-Euro countries have additional exchange rate volatility.
